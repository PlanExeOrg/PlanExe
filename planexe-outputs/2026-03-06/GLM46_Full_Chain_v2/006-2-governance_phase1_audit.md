
## Audit - Corruption Risks

- Bribery of local officials for faster permitting
- Nepotism in hiring local partners or suppliers
- Conflicts of interest with local business owners for partnership deals
- Kickbacks from supplier contracts for premium beans or equipment
- Information misuse to gain competitive advantage in local market

## Audit - Misallocation Risks

- Budget allocation for marketing vs. operational costs
- Time allocation for community engagement activities vs. core business operations
- Resource allocation for local sourcing initiatives vs. scaling production capacity
- Operational resource allocation between direct-to-consumer channels and wholesale partnerships

## Audit - Procedures

- Periodic review of supplier contracts and pricing agreements
- Quarterly financial statement audits focusing on cost variances and profit margins
- Regular equipment performance audits for roasting systems to ensure consistent quality standards
- Annual compliance audit of all permits, licenses, and regulatory requirements for commercial roasting operations

## Audit - Transparency Measures

- Publicly accessible dashboard displaying current sales volume, bean sourcing percentages, and operational metrics
- Regular publication of meeting minutes for governing bodies (e.g., local business association, supplier council) focusing on partnership decisions and supply chain management
- Implementation of a whistleblower mechanism for reporting potential corruption or unethical behavior in the project, including supplier relationships and operational practices
- Publicly available policies and procedures for sourcing local beans, pricing transparency, and quality standards, including clear definitions of acceptable practices and reporting mechanisms for non-compliance
- Establishment of a community engagement platform (e.g., local business directory, social media groups) to share project updates, sourcing information, and operational progress with rural Connecticut stakeholders
