# Identify Risks


## Risk 1 - Regulatory & Permitting
The operation of autonomous or semi-autonomous drones in urban-adjacent environments faces strict FAA Part 107 and Part 89 (Remote ID) regulations, as well as local municipal noise and privacy ordinances.

**Impact:** Grounding of the entire fleet or denial of operating permits, leading to a total loss of revenue for the affected site and potential legal fines. Estimated delay: 3–6 months for compliance re-certification.

**Likelihood:** High

**Severity:** High

**Action:** Engage local aviation counsel early; implement geofencing software that is hard-coded to site boundaries; maintain active communication with local FAA Flight Standards District Offices (FSDO).

## Risk 2 - Technical & Operational
The 'Pragmatic Foundation' relies on semi-autonomous systems and wearable haptic feedback. High-speed drone flight in a modular urban-mimicry arena increases the risk of signal interference, latency in proximity sensors, and hardware collisions.

**Impact:** Physical damage to drones, injury to players, and system downtime. Financial impact: $2,000–$5,000 per major collision event in hardware replacement and potential insurance premium hikes.

**Likelihood:** High

**Severity:** Medium

**Action:** Implement redundant, non-GPS-dependent positioning systems (e.g., UWB or optical flow); conduct rigorous stress testing of collision-avoidance algorithms in a 'dry run' environment before public launch.

## Risk 3 - Financial & Market
High capital expenditure for modular infrastructure and specialized drone hardware creates a high 'break-even' threshold. If customer acquisition costs (CAC) in urban centers like LA or Austin exceed projections, the tiered membership model may fail to cover fixed costs.

**Impact:** Cash flow insolvency within the first 12–18 months of operation. Potential loss of initial capital investment ($250k–$500k per site).

**Likelihood:** Medium

**Severity:** High

**Action:** Prioritize corporate team-building events as a high-margin, low-CAC revenue stream to subsidize individual memberships; maintain a lean, modular infrastructure design that can be scaled down if demand is lower than expected.

## Risk 4 - Supply Chain
Reliance on specialized, ruggedized drone components and wearable haptic electronics creates vulnerability to supply chain disruptions, especially if components are proprietary or sourced from limited vendors.

**Impact:** Extended downtime for the fleet (2–4 weeks) due to inability to source spare parts, leading to missed bookings and damage to brand reputation.

**Likelihood:** Medium

**Severity:** Medium

**Action:** Maintain a 20% buffer of critical spare parts (motors, propellers, sensors) on-site; diversify component sourcing to avoid single-vendor dependency.

## Risk 5 - Social & Safety
The perception of 'military-style' drone pursuit in public or semi-public spaces may trigger community backlash or 'nuisance' complaints regarding noise and privacy.

**Impact:** Public relations crisis, negative media coverage, and potential zoning restrictions imposed by local councils. Could force a relocation of the arena.

**Likelihood:** Medium

**Severity:** Medium

**Action:** Implement strict noise-reduction protocols (e.g., low-noise propellers); conduct community outreach programs; ensure all flight paths are strictly contained within the private arena perimeter.

## Risk summary
The project's success is most critically threatened by **Regulatory/FAA compliance** and **Operational Safety/Liability**. Because the project involves flying drones near humans, any failure in the 'semi-autonomous' logic or physical safety barriers could result in catastrophic injury, leading to immediate shutdown and potential litigation. The third critical risk is **Financial Sustainability**; the high capital intensity of the 'Pragmatic Foundation' requires high throughput, making the business sensitive to fluctuations in customer demand. Mitigation strategies must prioritize safety-first engineering and a diversified revenue stream (corporate vs. individual) to ensure the business remains resilient against both regulatory scrutiny and market volatility.

## Issue 1 - Missing Assumption: Insurance Market Viability
The plan assumes insurance is a manageable cost, but it fails to account for the 'insurability' of a novel, high-risk activity involving autonomous drones flying near humans. Standard commercial liability policies often exclude 'unmanned aerial systems' or 'high-risk recreational activities.'

**Recommendation:** Conduct a formal 'Insurance Feasibility Study' before committing to site leases. Engage a specialty broker to secure a preliminary quote. If premiums exceed 15-20% of projected annual revenue, the business model must be adjusted to include higher safety-tier hardware (e.g., full-cage drones) to lower risk profiles.

**Sensitivity:** If insurance is unavailable or premiums are 3x higher than standard recreational rates (baseline: 5% of revenue), the project's ROI could drop by 10-15%, potentially rendering the venture non-viable.

## Issue 2 - Missing Assumption: Latency and Interference in Dense Urban Environments
The plan assumes a LAN will solve latency issues. However, urban-adjacent industrial sites are often saturated with 2.4GHz/5.8GHz noise from neighboring businesses, which can cause 'fly-aways' or signal drops in semi-autonomous drones, leading to safety incidents.

**Recommendation:** Mandate a professional RF (Radio Frequency) site survey for every location. Implement a dedicated, licensed, or private 5G/CBRS network for drone-to-server communication to ensure zero-latency command and control, rather than relying on standard Wi-Fi/LAN.

**Sensitivity:** A 100ms increase in latency (baseline: <20ms) could lead to a 20-30% increase in hardware collision rates, increasing annual maintenance costs by $15,000–$30,000 per site.

## Issue 3 - Missing Assumption: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) vs. Churn
The plan assumes a tiered membership model will drive recurring revenue. However, it lacks an assumption regarding the 'novelty decay' rate. High-tech entertainment often sees a massive drop-off after the first visit if the 'combat' experience is not constantly updated with new scenarios.

**Recommendation:** Budget for a 'Content Refresh' cycle (new game modes, map layouts, or drone software updates) every 3 months. Allocate 10% of annual revenue to R&D for software-based scenario updates to maintain a 40%+ repeat-customer rate.

**Sensitivity:** If the repeat-customer rate falls below 25% (baseline: 40%), the Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) drops by 35%, potentially extending the break-even point from 18 months to 30+ months.

## Review conclusion
The project is highly ambitious but currently relies on 'best-case' assumptions regarding insurance, RF stability, and customer retention. The three most critical risks—insurability, technical signal interference, and novelty decay—must be addressed through proactive RF surveys, specialty insurance underwriting, and a dedicated content-refresh budget. Addressing these will shift the project from a high-risk gamble to a defensible, scalable business model.
