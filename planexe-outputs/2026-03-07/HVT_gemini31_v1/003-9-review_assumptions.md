Project Management & Operational Risk Strategy

- Human-Drone Interaction (HDI) Safety Standards

## Issue 1 - Missing Assumption: Insurance Market Viability
The plan assumes insurance is a manageable cost, but it fails to account for the 'insurability' of a novel, high-risk activity involving autonomous drones flying near humans. Standard commercial liability policies often exclude 'unmanned aerial systems' or 'high-risk recreational activities.'

**Recommendation:** Conduct a formal 'Insurance Feasibility Study' before committing to site leases. Engage a specialty broker to secure a preliminary quote. If premiums exceed 15-20% of projected annual revenue, the business model must be adjusted to include higher safety-tier hardware (e.g., full-cage drones) to lower risk profiles.

**Sensitivity:** If insurance is unavailable or premiums are 3x higher than standard recreational rates (baseline: 5% of revenue), the project's ROI could drop by 10-15%, potentially rendering the venture non-viable.

## Issue 2 - Missing Assumption: Latency and Interference in Dense Urban Environments
The plan assumes a LAN will solve latency issues. However, urban-adjacent industrial sites are often saturated with 2.4GHz/5.8GHz noise from neighboring businesses, which can cause 'fly-aways' or signal drops in semi-autonomous drones, leading to safety incidents.

**Recommendation:** Mandate a professional RF (Radio Frequency) site survey for every location. Implement a dedicated, licensed, or private 5G/CBRS network for drone-to-server communication to ensure zero-latency command and control, rather than relying on standard Wi-Fi/LAN.

**Sensitivity:** A 100ms increase in latency (baseline: <20ms) could lead to a 20-30% increase in hardware collision rates, increasing annual maintenance costs by $15,000–$30,000 per site.

## Issue 3 - Missing Assumption: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) vs. Churn
The plan assumes a tiered membership model will drive recurring revenue. However, it lacks an assumption regarding the 'novelty decay' rate. High-tech entertainment often sees a massive drop-off after the first visit if the 'combat' experience is not constantly updated with new scenarios.

**Recommendation:** Budget for a 'Content Refresh' cycle (new game modes, map layouts, or drone software updates) every 3 months. Allocate 10% of annual revenue to R&D for software-based scenario updates to maintain a 40%+ repeat-customer rate.

**Sensitivity:** If the repeat-customer rate falls below 25% (baseline: 40%), the Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) drops by 35%, potentially extending the break-even point from 18 months to 30+ months.

## Review conclusion
The project is highly ambitious but currently relies on 'best-case' assumptions regarding insurance, RF stability, and customer retention. The three most critical risks—insurability, technical signal interference, and novelty decay—must be addressed through proactive RF surveys, specialty insurance underwriting, and a dedicated content-refresh budget. Addressing these will shift the project from a high-risk gamble to a defensible, scalable business model.
