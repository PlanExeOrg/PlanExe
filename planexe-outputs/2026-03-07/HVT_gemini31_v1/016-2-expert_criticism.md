## 1.4.A Issue - Regulatory Naivety Regarding 'Autonomous' Operations

You are conflating 'automated' with 'autonomous' in a way that will trigger an immediate FAA enforcement action. Under current Part 107 rules, the FAA does not recognize 'fully autonomous' flight in proximity to humans as a standard recreational activity. Your reliance on 'hardware-level kill switches' as a primary safety defense is a reactive, not proactive, legal strategy. The FSDO will require a formal Part 107 waiver (or a Part 135-style safety management system) for operations involving autonomous flight over human participants.

### 1.4.B Tags

- FAA Compliance
- Regulatory Risk
- Safety Management

### 1.4.C Mitigation

Consult with an aviation attorney specializing in Part 107 waivers. Shift your documentation from 'autonomous' to 'pilot-in-command (PIC) supervised automation.' Read FAA Advisory Circular 107-2A and research the 'Safety Case' requirements for operations over human beings (14 CFR § 107.39).

### 1.4.D Consequence

Immediate cease-and-desist order from the local FSDO, resulting in total loss of seed capital and potential permanent blacklisting from commercial drone operations.

### 1.4.E Root Cause

Over-reliance on technical solutions (kill switches) to solve legal/regulatory hurdles.

## 1.5.A Issue - Insurance Underwriting Delusion

You have budgeted 15% of revenue for insurance, but you have not identified a carrier willing to underwrite 'autonomous drone-human combat.' Most standard aviation liability policies explicitly exclude 'intentional contact' or 'autonomous flight in proximity to non-participants.' You are currently building a business model on a foundation of sand; if you cannot secure a binder, the project is dead on arrival.

### 1.5.B Tags

- Insurance
- Liability
- Financial Viability

### 1.5.C Mitigation

Stop all capital expenditure. Engage a broker who specializes in 'Aviation/Aerospace Liability' (e.g., Global Aerospace, Allianz, or specialized Lloyd's syndicates). Provide them with your 'Safety Case' and 'Hardware Failsafe' documentation immediately. If they cannot quote, you must pivot to non-contact, tethered, or simulated-only drone interactions.

### 1.5.D Consequence

Inability to secure a lease for any commercial property and total personal liability for the founders in the event of a minor injury.

### 1.5.E Root Cause

Failure to validate the 'insurability' of the core business model before planning the operational infrastructure.

## 1.6.A Issue - Operational Throughput vs. Safety Bottleneck

Your plan assumes high throughput, but you have ignored the 'Safety/Range Officer' (RO) overhead. In a high-stakes, autonomous environment, you will need a 1:1 or 1:2 RO-to-player ratio to maintain safety compliance. This destroys your labor cost projections. You are treating this like a paintball field, but the regulatory and safety oversight required for drones makes this closer to a flight school or a high-risk industrial site.

### 1.6.B Tags

- Operational Costs
- Labor Efficiency
- Throughput

### 1.6.C Mitigation

Conduct a 'Time and Motion' study for a single session. Calculate the cost of a certified Range Officer per hour. Re-evaluate your pricing model to reflect the high labor-to-player ratio. Consult with existing drone-racing league operators (e.g., MultiGP) to understand their safety-to-throughput ratios.

### 1.6.D Consequence

The business will fail to reach break-even within 18 months because the labor costs required to keep the arena 'safe' will exceed the revenue generated by the sessions.

### 1.6.E Root Cause

Underestimating the human-in-the-loop requirement for safety-critical autonomous systems.

## 2.3 Follow Up Consultation

We need to review your RF propagation study and the specific FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) for your drone fleet. Bring your proposed CBRS network topology and the specific insurance policy language you are negotiating.

## 2.4.A Issue - Naive Reliance on 'Hardware-Level' Safety

You are placing excessive faith in hardware-level kill switches and proximity sensors as a primary safety strategy. In an RF-congested environment, a 'kill switch' is only as good as the link budget supporting it. If your command-and-control (C2) link is compromised by interference, your 'fail-safe' becomes a 'fail-deadly' scenario where the drone continues its last commanded trajectory without oversight.

### 2.4.B Tags

- Safety
- C2 Link
- Systemic Risk

### 2.4.C Mitigation

Implement a 'Dead-Man's Switch' architecture where the drone enters a 'Hover-and-Land' state automatically upon loss of heartbeat signal, independent of the primary flight controller. Consult with a functional safety engineer (ISO 26262 standards) to perform a Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA).

### 2.4.D Consequence

A single lost-link event resulting in a collision with a player will lead to immediate FAA grounding, permanent loss of insurance, and likely criminal liability.

### 2.4.E Root Cause

Over-reliance on software-defined safety logic without accounting for physical-layer link degradation.

## 2.5.A Issue - Underestimation of RF Interference in Urban Arenas

Your plan assumes a private 5G/CBRS network will solve all latency issues. You have not accounted for multipath fading caused by the 'urban-mimicry' obstacles (shipping containers/scaffolding). These metal structures will create significant signal reflections, leading to destructive interference and 'dead zones' that will cause your autonomous swarm-AI to jitter or crash.

### 2.5.B Tags

- RF Interference
- Latency
- Infrastructure

### 2.5.C Mitigation

Perform a 3D ray-tracing propagation study of your arena layout before construction. Deploy a multi-node CBRS small-cell architecture with beamforming to ensure consistent coverage around obstacles. Read: 'CBRS Spectrum Sharing and Interference Mitigation' whitepapers from the Wireless Innovation Forum.

### 2.5.D Consequence

Unpredictable drone behavior, high packet loss, and failure to meet the 20ms latency requirement, rendering the 'high-immersion' experience stuttery and broken.

### 2.5.E Root Cause

Failure to model the physical environment's impact on RF propagation.

## 2.6.A Issue - Operational Throughput vs. Maintenance Reality

You are planning for high-throughput, but your maintenance strategy is reactive. Industrial-grade drones are not 'set and forget.' The vibration profiles of high-speed pursuit drones will cause fatigue in carbon-fiber frames and sensor mounts. Your 20% spare parts buffer is optimistic; you need a full 'hot-swap' fleet rotation strategy.

### 2.6.B Tags

- Maintenance
- Operational Throughput
- Hardware

### 2.6.C Mitigation

Adopt a 'Flight-Hour-Based' maintenance schedule rather than a reactive one. Implement automated pre-flight diagnostic routines that check motor torque and sensor calibration before every session. Provide data on expected MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures) for your specific airframe choice.

### 2.6.D Consequence

Operational downtime will exceed 30%, causing revenue targets to collapse and destroying the 'tiered membership' value proposition.

### 2.6.E Root Cause

Lack of a rigorous, data-driven maintenance lifecycle plan.
