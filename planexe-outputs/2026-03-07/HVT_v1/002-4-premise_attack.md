### Premise Attack 1 — Integrity
_Forensic audit of foundational soundness across axes._

**[MORAL] Commercializing the simulation of being hunted by sovereign military assets trivializes lethal targeting protocols and erodes public trust in security apparatuses.**

**Bottom Line:** REJECT: The premise violates safety norms and trivializes military targeting, making it legally unviable and morally bankrupt.


#### Reasons for Rejection

- Simulating the role of a High Value Target for the US military turns real-world lethal designation into recreational entertainment, trivializing the gravity of state-sanctioned elimination protocols.
- Deploying autonomous drones instead of paintball guns in civilian outdoor arenas creates an unregulated airspace hazard where pursuit logic overrides standard safety protocols inherent to recreational games.
- The premise blurs the line between military targeting and civilian recreation, risking public desensitization to drone warfare imagery and tactics.

#### Second-Order Effects

- 0–6 months: Local jurisdictions ban autonomous recreational flight due to safety incidents or airspace violations during initial venue testing.
- 1–3 years: Civilian distrust grows as military drone imagery becomes associated with commercial leisure rather than defense operations.
- 5–10 years: Regulatory frameworks struggle to distinguish between licensed military training and civilian entertainment using similar autonomous systems.

#### Evidence

- Law/Standard — U.S. Federal Aviation Administration Regulations (Part 107, 2016): Requires visual line of sight and prohibits autonomous operation over crowds.
- Case/Incident — London Gatwick Airport Disruption (2019): Demonstrates how civilian airspace security collapses under autonomous aerial threats.
