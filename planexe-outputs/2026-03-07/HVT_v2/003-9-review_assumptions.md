## Issue 1 - Insurance Liability Feasibility Assumption
The plan implicitly assumes that commercial drone liability insurers will accept the risk profile of high-speed 'combat' drones at standard rates. Insurers often classify this as extreme hazard, potentially requiring specialized coverage or denying policies entirely.

**Recommendation:** Secure binding insurance quotes before signing lease agreements. Budget for a minimum $50k/year premium contingency. Engage a broker specializing in drone liability to validate risk acceptance criteria.

**Sensitivity:** If premiums exceed $45,000/year (baseline: $20,000), annual net profit margin drops by 8-12%. If coverage is denied, project viability collapses immediately (ROI impact: -100%).

## Issue 2 - Novelty Fatigue & Retention Assumption
The plan assumes repeat visitation rates sufficient to sustain franchise growth without aggressive continuous marketing spend. Physical novelty venues often suffer high churn after the initial launch phase.

**Recommendation:** Implement a loyalty program and seasonal event updates immediately. Track week-over-week retention data. Target a minimum 15% monthly return rate to validate unit economics before franchising.

**Sensitivity:** If repeat visitation drops below 5% (baseline: 15%), Customer Acquisition Cost payback extends from 4 months to 12 months, requiring an additional $200,000 in working capital.

## Issue 3 - Hardware Lead Time Stability Assumption
The plan assumes custom sensors and drones will procure within standard timelines (90 days). Global supply chain volatility for electronics could cause significant delays.

**Recommendation:** Order critical hardware components 6 months prior to construction completion. Secure backup suppliers or stockpile inventory during fit-out phase to mitigate launch delays.

**Sensitivity:** A 4-month delay in hardware delivery pushes launch into Q4, reducing Year 1 revenue by 25% ($75,000 loss) and delaying ROI realization by 6 months.

## Review conclusion
The HVT venture faces critical risks in insurance viability, customer retention, and supply chain resilience that are not fully quantified in the current assumptions. To ensure success, immediate action is required to secure binding insurance terms, validate repeat visitation rates through pilot testing, and buffer hardware procurement timelines against global volatility. Failure to address these missing assumptions could extend break-even by 12+ months or render the business model uninsurable.
