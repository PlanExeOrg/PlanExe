## Focus and Context
The world’s first FAA-certified commercial drone combat simulation venue merges military-grade technology with safe, immersive gameplay. This venture aims to pioneer a scalable national brand that redefines team dynamics and tactical sport through rigorous regulatory adherence.

## Purpose and Goals
Launch HVT venues in Atlanta, Dallas-Fort Worth, and Pittsburgh by April 2027; achieve break-even in Year 2; secure FAA Part 107 compliance; and establish franchise viability with a target of three regional LOIs within 24 months post-launch.

## Key Deliverables and Outcomes

- Three industrial warehouse venues fitted for year-round drone operations
- FAA Part 107 licenses and binding insurance coverage secured
- Custom collision sensor integration ensuring player safety
- Corporate team-building packages and franchise licensing framework operational

## Timeline and Budget
Launch targeted for April 2027 following a 3-6 month regulatory approval window. Initial CAPEX is $500,000 per location with a $50,000/year insurance premium contingency fund required before lease signing.

## Risks and Mitigations

- Regulatory & Insurance Interdependency: Conflating pilot licensing with aircraft airworthiness could invalidate coverage; Mitigation requires immediate Part 107 Subpart E gap analysis and binding insurance LOIs prior to hardware procurement.
- Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities: Data-at-rest encryption is insufficient for RF control channels; Mitigation involves implementing Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) and third-party penetration testing during development.

## Audience Tailoring
Tailored for Venture Capitalists and Strategic Investors seeking scalable entertainment ventures with defensible moats, emphasizing safety-first compliance as a competitive advantage rather than an operational obstacle.

## Action Orientation

## Overall Takeaway
HVT transforms regulatory risk into a competitive moat through safety-first compliance, positioning the venture as a scalable national franchisor of experiences rather than a single-location operator.
