
## Question Answer Pair 1
**Question**: What is the critical regulatory distinction between obtaining a Part 107 pilot license and ensuring the drone hardware itself is legally certified for operations near people?
**Answer**: There is a significant confusion in the plan between operator certification and airworthiness. A FAA Part 107 license certifies the *pilot* to fly, but it does not automatically authorize the specific drone aircraft to operate over people without separate airworthiness certification (Part 107 Subpart E) or a special waiver. The document notes that relying on custom collision sensors is insufficient for legal clearance; the hardware itself must be verified as compliant for over-person operations to avoid immediate cease-and-desist orders and fines.
**Rationale**: This Q&A clarifies a critical regulatory misconception identified in the Expert Review (Issue 1.4). Understanding this distinction is vital because assuming the Part 107 license covers the aircraft risks operational shutdowns, fines up to $27,000 per violation, and invalidates insurance coverage, which are existential threats to the venture's legal existence.
